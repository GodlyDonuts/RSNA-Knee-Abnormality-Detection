The supervision claims match the pinned verified release: 21,044 observed training cells; seven MCL, nine Baker's and five fracture positives; fifteen synovitis negatives; one weak-validation positive each for MCL, Baker's and fracture. The release hash remains `6453a8ff31373c40556dc857e15fa0025f122195388a2b46c81ecd9cff0b8833`. Counts justify shared features and regularized heads, not optimistic claims that more architecture search will solve rare-target estimation. Existing `masked_bce` excludes zero-observation target terms and leaves a zero-loss path for fully masked batches. Any new accumulation, weighting or auxiliary-training implementation still needs its own denominator, leakage and masked-gradient checks.

The current version already includes valid DINOv2 input sizes (224 or 252 for fourteen-pixel patches), group-balanced sampling as a controlled comparison, and square-root weights capped at five and normalized within target. Related-study groups must remain intact for all fitted preprocessing, representation learning, teachers and ensemble fitting. Fixed, unfitted pretrained features may be cached once; fold-specific learned encoders or normalization cannot be reused across held-out folds. All-masked studies cannot silently become supervised negatives. Report/image correspondence holds remain binding for contrastive pairs as well as discrete labels.

**Budget clarification, now resolved in the final plan.** The plan's 4 + 8 + 48 + 12 hour starter totals 72 hours correctly. At its illustrative 0.5–2 seconds per study, one twenty-epoch run over 3,403 studies costs 9.45–37.81 GPU-hours before validation. Three folds and two seeds, with each fold training on approximately two-thirds of those studies, imply roughly four full-run equivalents per candidate: 37.81–151.24 hours before validation, final retraining or failed runs. Therefore the 48-hour fine-tuning component cannot promise the full multi-candidate fold/seed matrix. Use cheap frozen-feature fold comparisons first; after the pilot, predeclare the affordable number of fine-tuned candidates, epochs and replications. Move exhaustive comparisons to the separately proposed broader search or stop. The final strategy explicitly restricts the starter to a screen and selected replication, assigns full comparisons only where the measured budget permits, and treats unfinished comparisons as inconclusive. This resolves my scope concern; the price arithmetic was already correct.

The six-hour cold-inference target is a sensible, more conservative alternative to my memo's seven hours: approximately 16.62 seconds per study for 1,300 studies, versus a nine-hour maximum of 24.92 seconds per study. Neither is a measured model runtime. Retain full DICOM decoding, model loading, all ensemble members, CSV generation and high-series-count tails in the benchmark. Memory and 120–160 GB GPU-workspace storage estimates remain assumptions to verify; maintain the 20 GiB free-space floor and include unpacking and checkpoint temporaries.

Source checks support the limited claims made. [Torchvision](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models/generated/torchvision.models.convnext_tiny.html) lists 28,589,128 parameters for ConvNeXt-Tiny, consistent with “roughly 29 million”; its stock central crop should not override the plan's complete-knee-field requirement. [DINOv3's repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3) lists small/base ViTs and ConvNeXt variants, and [its license](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/blob/main/LICENSE.md) has distinct redistribution terms; the plan correctly treats it as optional pending exact eligibility checks. [Swin UNETR pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14791) used 5,050 CT images and segmentation evaluations, so the stated CT-to-knee limitation is accurate. [MAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377), [ConVIRT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00747) and [FixMatch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685) motivate objectives but do not establish gains for this sparse twelve-target knee setting. My original memo separately documents the MRNet, attention-MIL and DINOv2 primary checks.

The [provider page](https://www.runpod.io/pricing), independently viewed September 22, displayed RTX 4090 at $0.74/hour, A100 PCIe 80 GB at $1.59/hour and standard network storage under 1 TB at $0.07/GB/month. The arithmetic is correct: 72 hours costs $53.28 or $114.48 at those respective rates; 250–400 hours at $0.74 costs $185–296, and 150 GB storage is $10.50/month. These are visible examples, not available reserved hardware or a complete invoice.

The success condition remains repeatable improvement under grouped evaluation, credible per-target behavior, preserved supervision safeguards and successful offline inference within budget. The plan can reject unhelpful complexity and can return an inconclusive result. That makes it a substantive research strategy rather than a promise to win.
