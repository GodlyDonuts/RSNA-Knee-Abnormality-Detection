Author: `/root/competition_contract` · 2026-09-22 · Planning only; no weights, datasets or paid compute acquired.

**Start with a pretrained 2D encoder and hierarchical, sequence-aware multiple-instance learning (MIL), then test adjacent-slice 2.5D context. Make a compact 3D model a controlled challenger.** The immediate constraint is credible supervision and evaluation, particularly rare targets, rather than model size. This recommendation is a hypothesis; no competitive performance or rank has been measured.

That establishes execution correctness, not diagnostic accuracy. The final release contains 4,407 studies / 4,255 groups: 3,904 training studies, 441 weak-validation studies, 58 historically exposed gold studies and four quarantined studies. Training has 3,403 studies with any observed target, in 3,761 total training groups; weak validation has 394 with any observed target, in 436 groups. Working constraints are the coordinator's current official-rule verification, not a new rules check: mean of twelve AUCs, offline inference within nine hours, approximately 1,300 hidden studies without Report, five submissions per day / two final selections, October 15 entry and October 22 final deadline at 23:59 UTC; KneeCoT remains prohibited. **Starting model.** Compare two initializations only: ImageNet ConvNeXt-Tiny and DINOv2 ViT-S/14. First freeze each encoder and train identical small pooling heads; then fine-tune only the more promising configuration, initially its final blocks. ConvNeXt supplies a modern convolutional baseline; DINOv2 supplies transferable image features, with a 384-dimensional small-model embedding. Neither source demonstrates superiority on this competition. Use 224-pixel input initially; DINO's dimensions must respect its fourteen-pixel patch size. [ConvNeXt paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545), [DINOv2 official model card](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2/blob/main/MODEL_CARD.md).

Encode each series separately, add physical slice-position and spacing-validity features, pool slices with a small attention module, then pool series with twelve target-specific queries. Supply plane and coarse sequence/contrast indicators with explicit unknown values; do not substitute literal side tags for anatomical truth. Preserve variable series counts and missing sequences. For the initial budget, sample up to six eligible series and sixteen evenly distributed slices per series, rotating training coverage. Compare this with denser sampling before trusting focal-lesion sensitivity; inference should cover all eligible series where measured runtime permits. Keep series selection deterministic at inference and independent of reports or labels. Sequence dropout should test tolerance to missing protocols.

A plain max/mean pooling baseline is essential. MRNet demonstrated pretrained slice encoders and multi-plane fusion for knee MRI, but its three tasks and dataset differ from these twelve targets. Attention MIL offers a learnable bag aggregator; attention weights are not validated lesion localization. [MRNet primary study](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1002699), [attention MIL paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ilse18a.html).

The next context experiment stacks three neighboring slices from the same ordered series; compare physical-offset sampling with index adjacency where spacing permits. Never stack unregistered planes as if spatially aligned. A compact 3D ResNet challenger processes individual series or modest slabs, followed by the same study-level fusion. It may capture morphology better, but anisotropic sampling, field variation, interpolation and activation memory can offset that advantage. MedicalNet provides pretrained 3D ResNets; its transfer evidence does not establish knee-specific benefit or fair comparability with our 2D setup. Compare equal input coverage and measured compute, and report whether any gain comes from more slices rather than architecture. [Med3D paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00625), [official MedicalNet repository](https://github.com/Tencent/MedicalNet).

**Loss and scarce targets.** Begin with masked binary cross-entropy, normalized separately by each target's observed cells and then averaged across active targets. Skip zero-observation target terms safely. Sample training groups before studies so related examinations do not dominate. Keep the 501 training studies without observed targets out of supervised loss; only technically eligible training images may enter a separately declared self-supervised experiment. Gold and validation images remain outside any fitted pretraining stage.

Test one bounded balancing change: class weights derived from training-only observed group counts, square-root rather than raw inverse frequency, capped at five and normalized within target. Treat synovitis's scarce negatives as carefully as fracture's scarce positives. Cap repeated rare-study sampling; duplication creates no new independent evidence. Share the encoder, regularize heads, and report leave-one-positive-group-out sensitivity for fracture/MCL/Baker's and leave-one-negative-group-out sensitivity for synovitis. These are fragility checks, not precise performance estimates.

Do not convert masked labels to negatives, equate report silence with absence, or undo severity/correspondence holds to increase sample counts. CheXpert found that uncertainty treatments could differ by pathology; that supports controlled testing, not importing a chest-label policy into knees. ASL is an optional later loss ablation; its suppression of easy negatives is especially questionable for our negative-scarce synovitis head. Standard positive-unlabeled learning also needs defensible sampling/prior assumptions: report omission and severity-dependent masking are not evidently random. Avoid PU or pseudo-labeling as the initial solution. If expert image adjudication is feasible and permitted later, prioritize additional training-only rare positives and synovitis negatives with explicit competition definitions, rather than relaxing report rules. [CheXpert study](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/3834), [ASL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14119), [non-negative PU paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/7cce53cf90577442771720a370c3c723-Abstract.html).

**Evaluation discipline.** Freeze existing related-study groups. Create three fixed grouped inner folds within training, with target-aware stratification where possible; pooled out-of-fold predictions guide the small experiment set. Fit preprocessing, weighting, any teacher and any learned fusion entirely within each fold. A fold lacking either class has undefined AUC, never zero or an invented 0.5. Five fracture positives cannot support reliable five-fold model selection.

Use the locked 441-study weak-validation partition only at predefined milestones. Report all twelve observed-label AUCs with class/group counts, coverage, per-target loss and grouped bootstrap uncertainty; disclose invalid bootstrap replicates. AUC with one positive describes the rank of one examination, and resampling cannot create positive diversity. A predeclared secondary diagnostic across ACL, both menisci and contusion is more stable here, but is not the competition metric and must never replace the twelve-target report. Weak labels measure agreement with selected report evidence, not a clean hidden-test AUC estimate.

The 58 gold studies are historically exposed and must not be described as untouched. Keep them evaluation-only; no gradients, threshold fitting, loss-weight selection, checkpoint selection or stacking against them. Use a limited, preregistered diagnostic audit after architecture choices. We do not currently possess a truly untouched, adequately powered local twelve-target image-reference test. The private leaderboard remains an external outcome, not a tuning dataset. Stratify errors by available scanner/manufacturer/model and acquisition features; report language is a supervision-quality stratum only. These are proxies, not verified site identities, and report language is unavailable to the deployed predictor.

**Prioritized experiments and decision gates.** Each row changes one principal factor; preserve folds, seeds, input coverage and release hashes.

| Order | Hypothesis / experiment | Evidence needed to continue |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Frozen encoder + max/mean MIL; ConvNeXt vs DINO-S | Correct masks and group isolation; finite twelve-output predictions; cold inference timing; no constant-head collapse. |
| 1 | Hierarchical attention + sequence indicators improves fusion | Positive paired inner-fold change, same direction across seeds; compare metadata-present and unknown-sequence strata. Reject a gain explained only by series count. |
| 2 | Fine-tuning improves MRI features | Improvement survives a second seed without materially worse generalization gap; otherwise retain frozen encoder. |
| 3 | More slice coverage / 2.5D improves focal findings | Compare sixteen vs thirty-two slices first, then context at fixed sampled centers. Require repeatable gains, not one rare validation example. |
| 4 | Bounded balancing helps scarce classes | Better out-of-fold rare-group rankings without common-target collapse; inspect sensitivity to removing each rare group. |
| 5 | Conservative supervision improves generalization | On identical validation labels, compare full accepted supervision with a predefined stricter high-confidence training subset; separately test excluding target-ambiguous studies entirely. Match observed-update budgets where possible. Never re-enable quarantined correspondence or replace masked cells. This tests incremental policy effects, not the entire historical cleaning claim. |
| 6 | Compact 3D adds complementary information | Beat or complement the chosen 2D model at a declared compute/coverage budget; otherwise stop. |

For advancement, use a practical screening threshold such as +0.01 in the predefined secondary diagnostic, consistent across at least two seeds and no unexplained >0.03 common-target regression. These are proposed resource-allocation rules, not significance claims or promised effect sizes. Examine paired group-bootstrap differences and per-target outcomes; if uncertainty spans material harm and benefit, favor the simpler model. The final choice must consider all twelve targets, robustness and runtime. Freeze this rule before scores are available.

**Compute and release plan.** Hardware is not assumed to be available: the planning envelope is one 24-GB NVIDIA GPU, mixed precision, one-study microbatches, encoder chunks and activation checkpointing when fine-tuning; benchmark memory first. Chunking forward passes alone does not eliminate retained backward activations. No paid allocation is authorized by this memo.

The verified uint16 cache is 82,555,132,978 bytes across 24,386 series / 819,635 slices; reuse it rather than duplicating volumes. One FP16 384-dimensional vector per cached slice would require about 629 MB before indexing; 768 dimensions require 1.259 GB. Cache only the current encoder's permitted feature split, with encoder/preprocessing hashes, and preserve the 20 GiB free-space floor including checkpoints and temporary files. Larger inputs interpolated from the 256-pixel cache add no native detail; a true high-resolution experiment needs a separately budgeted source-derived cache.

Cost the first run from measured end-to-end throughput: at a maximum 6×16 sampled images for each of 3,403 supervised studies, one epoch is at most 326,688 encoder instances. At an illustrative—not measured—100 training instances/second, that is 0.91 GPU-hours/epoch before validation and I/O; twenty epochs would be roughly 23 hours with 25% overhead. Recompute after the first benchmark and set a run cap. Currency cost is measured GPU-hours multiplied by the actual provider quote; no price is assumed. A sensible first allocation proposal is at most 48 GPU-hours for the two frozen probes and one selected fine-tuning/replication path; do not launch the full matrix if measured throughput makes that cap unrealistic.

Record immutable release/fold hashes, weight identity/license, software version, seed, sampler state, optimizer/scheduler/scaler/RNG state, per-target denominators, peak memory and cold runtime. The accepted resume check must remain part of each new training path. For hidden-test sizing, measure a cold, report-free end-to-end run including DICOM decoding, series selection, model load and CSV output; extrapolate to roughly 1,300 studies with margin and target no more than seven of the permitted nine hours. Benchmark failure cases and high-series-count tails, not just average cached throughput.

Only after individual models pass these gates, try an equal-weight two-model or two-seed blend using out-of-fold predictions. Avoid twelve independently tuned ensemble weights with this validation scarcity. Compare probability and rank averaging at a predefined milestone. Monotonic per-target calibration cannot improve an individual model's AUC; calibration is not an early objective. Add one deterministic TTA only if its incremental gain and full cold-runtime cost are justified. Select the two final submissions from predeclared evidence and meaningful model diversity, rather than using the daily quota as a hyperparameter search. No rank claim follows from clean data, a successful loader, or this plan.
