# Cartilage candidate inventory: discovery only

This inventory covers all 4,407 original report IDs and all 13,221 medial,
lateral and patellofemoral OA cells against the frozen report-weak-v2.0 rubric
and the 34 registered corrections. It reuses the complete qualified-wording
inventory's source/review coverage and cartilage candidates, then adds focused
mechanical retrieval indicators. It does not assign new clinical states.

The independent source-reading entry point is `neutral-packet-index.json` and
`neutral-full-report-packets/`. Each packet contains at most ten complete
original reports, source identifiers and hashes, with no annotations, target
names, trigger explanations or proposed corrections. Reports are ordered by
their original batch and index, not by a candidate severity score. Membership
is selected and is not a random or fully blinded sample.

Keep the separate detail files closed until a source assessment is frozen:

- `full-cartilage-coverage.jsonl` accounts for every report/target cell,
  including cells not selected, with its original and inspection-effective
  annotation and exact input bindings.
- `candidate-details.jsonl` records the selected cells, mechanical trigger IDs,
  evidence cues and references to the existing complete inventory.
- `source-cue-inventory.jsonl` preserves exact original-language cue matches,
  offsets and bounded contexts for each report. These are retrieval cues,
  not localized clinical conclusions.
- `input-coverage-and-reuse.json` binds all 89 source files and all 89 final
  reviewed files, the review index, rubric, registry, reused discovery files,
  and existing cartilage QA evidence.
- `summary.json` contains aggregate coverage and candidate counts;
  `selection-method.json` records the exact deterministic rule definitions.

Every effective positive OA cell is selected, even when its wording appears
to contain both depth and extent cues. Missing lexical cues are flags for
source reading, not proof that evidence is clinically unsupported. Every
observed negative with a numeric grade in its own evidence is retrieved for
framework/context review. A narrower flag identifies evidence with a grade,
no recognized framework anywhere in the report, and no non-grade depth cue
in that quotation; this is a proxy for grade-only justification, not a claim
that the full report lacks other valid support.

Mixed grade or severity cues retrieve possible contradictions without deciding
that they conflict. Different compartments, separate lesions, historical versus
current findings, and negation can explain their coexistence. No negation scope,
grading convention, cartilage compartment, depth fraction or maximum lesion
extent is clinically inferred by these rules. A short width measurement is not
converted into a maximum extent. Named frameworks are not automatically treated
as proving the frozen threshold.

Recall is limited: the lexical vocabulary covers common English, Spanish,
French, German, Turkish, Greek and some other European forms, not every language,
abbreviation, misspelling or paraphrase. Normalization is retrieval-only.
Source proximity can associate a meniscal or ligament grade with nearby cartilage;
absence of a recognized cue is not absence of a clinical fact. All positive
cells receive complete mechanical coverage independent of vocabulary, but other
semantic errors can remain outside the candidate set. Unselected rows are
accounted for, not certified correct.

Existing accepted corrections remain contextual evidence, not reopened errors.
The existing narrower cartilage audits and their actual scope are preserved.
The global cartilage QA gate remains pending. This inventory does not constitute
4,407 personally reread reports, independently adjudicated corrections, official
gold evaluation, or a training release. Gold, source reports, rubric, original
reviews, prior audit artifacts and runtime registries are not changed.

Run `../../.venv/bin/python ../annotation-staging/cartilage-global-candidate-inventory-v1/build_inventory.py`
from `work/recovered` to build once. Append `--verify` to reproduce the inventory
in memory and compare every generated byte with the preserved output. The build
refuses overwrites; verification is read-only except for its separately named
verification receipt when explicitly requested.
