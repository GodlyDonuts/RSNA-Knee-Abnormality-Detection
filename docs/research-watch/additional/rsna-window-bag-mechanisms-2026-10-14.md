## The useful distinction

**Raptor's random-window training is bag-level supervision, not a separate positive label applied to every sampled window.** Twelve triplets are jointly encoded; a different attention distribution is learned for each finding; twelve study-level logits are produced; BCE compares those logits with the study targets. The released source does not provide a lesion-presence guarantee for positive bags, lesion coordinates, or finding-specific sampling.

| Question | What the public source actually implements | Implication / limit |

Normally this samples12 distinct centers; centers repeat only when the candidate set is too small. `collate` returns one target vector per study; training calls `lossf(model(x), y)` after pooling | Joint bags avoid teaching every normal triplet independently that it is positive. A sampled positive study can still omit the lesion. No published positive-bag guarantee or measured miss rate |
| Max, mean or attention? | CoAtNet's spatial encoder uses average pooling within each triplet. Then LayerNorm features feed an F→256→12 attention network; softmax is overK windows separately for each finding. The head pools full-dimensional features, then applies a finding-specific linear classifier | This is neither hard-max MIL nor arithmetic averaging of window probabilities. Different findings can favor different windows. Attention is not proof of lesion localization, and no clean max-versus-mean-versus-attention score ablation was disclosed in the inspected material |

## Concrete ablation evidence, with limits

The previously verified [nartaa author table](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792) reports that reducing an earlier dense-window inference arm from94 to24 lowered public AUC from0.940 to0.934. This supports retaining coverage for that checkpoint; it does **not** isolate focal-lesion preservation or prove that every model benefits similarly. Smaller inference crops also hurt: the .948 no-mirror arm became .947 at304 and .946 at288. The exact biological reason was not established.

[Archit](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3525701) found masking/replacing noisy targets less useful than fractional blending. [Nicolai](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3529062) found the same50/50 bootstrap improved local CV but not the leaderboard. Neither result is a window-localization ablation.

## What changes the comparison with our pipeline

Our native model already has finding-specific attention and up to96 sampled grayscale images; “add attention” or “add more slices” is not a demonstrated gap. The source-slot candidate is a different case: training picks one3-slice group per slot, pools slots, and inference averages predictions across10 within-slot window positions. Raptor instead learns a **joint multi-window study bag** and applies the same type of bag head to a larger inference bag. That is the clearest disclosed aggregation/observation contract to test under matched targets and geometry. It is a hypothesis about our gap, not a demonstrated cause of the public-score difference.

Still genuinely undisclosed for the >.95 single-fold claims: exact bag size, per-plane selection, per-window loss, pooling implementation, positive-bag handling, pseudo-label teacher folds, any instance-level teacher signal, and matched focal/diffuse-class ablations.
